# Fact-checking

1. Extract every factual claim in the draft: numbers, dates, names, legal provisions, causal statements and citations. One claim per item, quoted from the draft.
2. Check each against the strongest source you can open (see the research rules), reading full pages and linked data files.
3. Verdicts:
   - supported: a source states it. Evidence quotes, in double quotes, the exact source words containing the claimed number, name or fact. No quote, no supported.
   - unsupported: no source found either way, or only a computed figure.
   - wrong: a credible source states otherwise; quote it.
   - sources disagree: credible sources give different values; quote each with its link and let the user decide.
4. Severity (high, medium, low) is how much the claim matters to the user's goal.
5. Give a suggested fix for every claim that is not supported: the corrected wording, with its source.
6. Tone: collaborative and specific. You are helping the researcher get it right, not scoring the writer.
