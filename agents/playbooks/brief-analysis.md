# Reading the brief

1. Read the whole brief together: project title, goal, what is needed, the deliverable, and any attached files. The title and goal often settle what the request leaves open.
2. Infer jurisdiction, time frame and scope from that context. Example: title "Quebec French Law", need "the new French laws and how English businesses are affected" means Quebec's Charter of the French Language as amended by Bill 96 (2022) and its later provisions, and English-speaking businesses operating in Quebec, not France or the UK.
3. Note the constraints the brief sets (audience, length, format, sources to use or avoid, date ranges).
4. When a term is still ambiguous, pick the most reasonable reading in context. Do not answer several readings at length.
5. State the reading in one line at the top, starting "Assumption:". Then answer fully.
6. Never stop to ask clarifying questions in a draft. The user can redraft with feedback.
